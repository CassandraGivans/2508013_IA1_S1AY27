7 Cures for a Lean Purse

The chapter "Seven Cures for a Lean Purse" explains different ways a person can improve their financial situation. The main lesson I took from the chapter is that building financial stability requires discipline, planning and consistent action.

Save at Least a Portion of Your Income

One of the most important lessons for me is the importance of saving. As a university student, I have to be mindful of how I use the money I receive because there are always expenses such as transportation, food, school supplies and other personal needs. Saving even a small amount can help me develop good financial habits.

Control Your Expenses

The chapter also taught me that earning money is not enough if I spend everything I receive. I need to distinguish between things I need and things I simply want. Learning to budget is important to me because I want to become more financially responsible while I am still young.

Make Your Money Grow

Another lesson is that saved money should eventually be used wisely to create more value. This encouraged me to think beyond simply having money and to consider how I can make better financial decisions in the future.

Protect Your Wealth

I also learned that money should not be placed into opportunities that I do not understand. Before making financial decisions, I should gain knowledge, ask people with experience and consider the risks involved.

5 Laws of Gold

The "Five Laws of Gold" explains the principles that can help a person keep and grow wealth. The chapter reinforced the idea that financial success is not only about getting money but also about knowing how to manage and protect it.

First Lesson: Save Consistently

The first lesson that stood out to me is that wealth begins with the habit of saving. I should not wait until I have a large amount of money before developing this habit. Starting with what I have is more realistic and can help me become disciplined.

Second Lesson: Make Wise Investments

The chapter also emphasizes putting money into places where it can safely grow. For me, this means learning about investments before making decisions instead of following something simply because other people are doing it.

Third Lesson: Seek Knowledge

Knowledge is important when dealing with money. As a computing student, I understand that I cannot become good at something without learning about it. The same principle applies to personal finance. I need to educate myself before making major financial decisions.

Fourth Lesson: Listen to Experienced People

Another important lesson is the value of advice from people who have experience. Instead of believing every financial opportunity I hear about, I should seek guidance from people who understand what they are doing.

Fifth Lesson: Protect Your Money

Finally, I learned that making money is only part of the process. I also need to protect it by avoiding unnecessary risks and making thoughtful decisions.

How These Lessons Relate to My Life

These chapters are relevant to me because I am at a stage in my life where I am learning how to become more independent and responsible. University has made me think more seriously about my future, my career and the type of life I want to build.

One of the biggest lessons I can apply is learning to budget my money instead of spending without a plan. I want to develop the habit of saving consistently, even when the amount is small. This can help me prepare for future goals while also teaching me discipline.

I also want to become more intentional about my career. My goal is to become a software developer, so investing in my education and developing valuable skills is another form of investment. The knowledge and experience I gain now can create opportunities for me in the future.

These lessons also remind me that financial growth takes time. I do not have to become wealthy immediately. Instead, I can focus on building good habits now and making better decisions as I grow.

My Biggest Takeaway

My biggest takeaway from both chapters is that financial success starts with discipline. Having money does not automatically mean that a person is financially successful. What matters is how the money is earned, managed, saved and used.

As a university student, I may not have a large income right now, but I can still develop the habits that I will need in the future. Saving, controlling my spending, learning about money and making wise decisions can help me build a stronger financial foundation.

These chapters taught me that I should start with what I have instead of waiting until I have more. The habits I develop today can influence the financial future I create for myself.
